# Why cities plant street trees

Trees planted along streets can make neighborhoods more comfortable during hot weather. Their leaves block some sunlight before it reaches pavement and building walls. Because shaded surfaces absorb less solar energy, they stay cooler and release less heat into the surrounding air later in the day.

Trees also move water from the soil to the air through transpiration. As that water evaporates from leaves, it uses heat energy and can cool nearby air. The effect depends on tree health, available water, canopy size, and local weather.

Street-tree programs involve tradeoffs. Young trees need watering and maintenance, roots need suitable soil space, and species must be selected for the local climate. City planners often combine temperature measurements with information about population vulnerability when deciding where new trees could provide the greatest benefit.

Trees are not a complete solution to urban heat. Reflective roofs, shaded transit stops, access to cooling centers, and building improvements can address needs that trees cannot. A strong heat plan combines multiple approaches.
